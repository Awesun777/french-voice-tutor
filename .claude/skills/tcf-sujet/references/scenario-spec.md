# What makes a valid TCF Canada — Tâche 2 sujet

Tâche 2 is the *exercice en interaction*: ~5 min 30 s in which the candidate must
**obtain information and negotiate** with the examiner, who plays a service
provider. The candidate is the one who has to drive — ask questions, compare,
choose, justify. The document on the sheet exists to give them something concrete
to ask about.

Sujet 1 (`logement`, the real `EO001IRN_demo` paper) is the reference for every
judgement below. Match its register and density, not its topic.

## The consigne

- Two or three short sentences, present tense, addressed to the candidate as
  **vous**.
- Sentence 1 names **Marc's** role: *« Je suis un agent immobilier. »*
- Sentence 2 names the candidate's **situation**: *« Vous cherchez un logement. »*
- Sentence 3 names the **speech act** required: *« Vous m'expliquez ce que vous
  cherchez. »* — explain, choose, book, complain, negotiate, cancel, enquire.
- No politeness formulas, no scene-setting, no "imaginez que". Flat and neutral.
- It is read aloud by Marc verbatim, so it must sound natural spoken.

## The role

- Marc is always the **professional** — the one with the information and the
  authority to say no. The candidate is always the **customer / user / applicant**.
- Everyday, low-stakes, culturally French service situations: restaurant, cabinet
  médical, gare/SNCF, banque, salle de sport, école, garagiste, agence de voyage,
  mairie, opérateur téléphonique, pressing, déménageur.
- Avoid anything requiring specialist vocabulary, bad news, medical detail, legal
  process, or emotional distress. The candidate is being assessed on interaction,
  not on knowing the word for a carburettor.

## The three options

The sheet always carries **exactly three** options. They must be:

- **Comparable** — same kind of thing, so choosing between them is meaningful.
- **Concretely detailed** — every option carries 2–4 hard facts drawn from the
  same axes (price, size, duration, time of day, distance, conditions,
  inclusions). Sujet 1 uses surface / location / furnished / rent.
- **Genuinely in tension** — no option should dominate. The cheapest is furthest
  away, the biggest is unfurnished, the most convenient costs more. This is what
  forces the candidate to ask follow-up questions instead of pointing.
- **Deliberately incomplete** — leave one axis unstated so the candidate has to
  ask for it. Sujet 1 does this with *« Loyer : voir avec l'agence »*, and that
  line (or its equivalent) should survive into every new sujet.

## Captions

Two or three lines of fragments, not sentences. No verbs, no final period.
Follow sujet 1's shape:

```
Studio 20 m², centre-ville
meublé, kitchenette
Loyer : voir avec l'agence
```

Line 1 = what it is + where. Line 2 = qualifiers. Line 3 = the withheld axis.

## French mechanics

- Real accents and typographic apostrophes (`l'agence`, not `l'agence` with a
  straight quote), `m²` with a superscript two, narrow spaces before `:` as the
  reference sheet has them.
- Prices in euros. Times on the 24-hour clock. Metric everywhere.
